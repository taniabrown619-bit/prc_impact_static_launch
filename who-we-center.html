Who We Center

The people closest to the issue often see what the data misses.

When a decision affects a community, demographics only tell part of the story. You also need to understand how people experience the issue, what shapes their views, and how policies, programs, and institutions show up in daily life.

That context gives decision makers a clearer picture of the people their work affects.

Where We Bring Particular Depth

Understanding people in context.

Our work brings particular depth to communities and public audiences whose perspectives are often central to public-impact decisions.

Black Communities and Families

We bring experience understanding how Black communities experience public decisions, institutions, and systems. Research can show what shapes trust, how people make sense of change, and what may be missed when decisions are made without their perspective.

Women, Mothers, and Caregivers

Women and caregivers often experience policies and programs through the realities of family, work, and responsibility. Research helps bring those experiences into the decisions being made.

Voters and Civic Audiences

We look beyond whether people support or oppose an issue. The goal is to understand how they are thinking about it, what shapes their views, and how public decisions are being understood.

Rural and Southern Communities

Place matters. Local history, relationships, institutions, and lived experience shape how people respond to policy, programs, and change. Research grounded in that context can surface what broader data may miss.

People Directly Affected by Public Systems

People closest to a policy, program, or system often see things decision makers do not. Their experiences can show how something is working in practice and where intent and reality may not line up.

What Context Adds

Who participates shapes what the research can reveal.

Good qualitative research considers more than the answer someone gives. It also considers the experience and context behind that answer.

RecruitmentReaching people whose experience is relevant to the question.

Research designAsking questions in language and sequences that make sense to participants.

FacilitationCreating the conditions for candor, trust, and more complete responses.

InterpretationUnderstanding what the findings mean within the environment that shaped them.

That matters when research is meant to inform a policy, program, message, investment, or public decision.
