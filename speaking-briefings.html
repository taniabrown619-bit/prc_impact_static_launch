Speaking & Briefings

A researcher who can tell your audience what people actually think.

Tania Johns is a qualitative researcher and civic strategist who has spent two decades in rooms with residents, voters, patients, and stakeholders — asking the questions institutions forget to ask before they make expensive, public, hard-to-reverse decisions.

She speaks to conferences, associations, leadership convenings, boards, and closed institutional audiences. Sessions are built on original fieldwork, not commentary.

Signature Talks

Each session can run as a keynote, a working session, or a private briefing. Content is adapted to the audience and the decision in front of them.

Nobody Asked Us: What Communities Actually Say About Data Centers

AI infrastructure is arriving in Southern towns faster than the public process built to absorb it. Cities are passing moratoria, residents are filing suit, and water systems are hitting capacity. This session separates what communities are actually saying from what both sides assume they are saying — and shows what a credible public input process looks like before a vote, not after.

For: municipal and county officials, planning and utility boards, chambers, economic development groups, environmental and civic organizations.

Before the Vote: Designing Community Input That Holds Up Under Pressure

Most engagement processes are built to be survivable, not useful. This session covers how to design listening that produces evidence a council, board, or funder can actually defend — how to recruit the people you are missing, what to ask, and how to know when the answer you got is real.

For: public agencies, planners, engagement and communications teams, coalitions preparing for a referendum or major decision.

The Trust Gap: Why Communities Say No to Things That Would Help Them

Nonparticipation is usually read as apathy. It is almost never apathy. Drawing on fieldwork across public investment, health, and public safety, this session reframes low turnout, low uptake, and low response as information about trust, access, and relevance — and shows what changes when institutions treat it that way.

For: foundations, nonprofit leadership, public health and health systems, civic and democracy organizations.

AI Literacy Isn't the Divide — Trust Is

The conversation about who gets left behind by AI focuses on skills and access. The harder problem is that people who have been surveilled, redlined, or misled by institutions are making a rational calculation, not an uninformed one. This session covers what that means for organizations deploying AI in services that communities cannot opt out of.

For: nonprofits and foundations, public agencies, higher education, civic technology and workforce audiences.

What Actually Gets Through: Writing for How People Really Read

Institutions write for themselves and wonder why nothing lands. Built on national research with prescribers and on message testing with voters and residents, this session shows the difference between what an organization intends to communicate and what an audience under time pressure actually takes in.

For: communications and marketing teams, health communicators, public information officers, association and membership audiences.

Buying Research Well

A practical session for the people who commission qualitative work: how to scope it, what a good RFP asks for, how to read a proposal, which corners cost you the most when cut, and how to tell whether findings are strong enough to act on.

For: procurement and program staff, grantmakers, executive directors, association professional development tracks.

Formats

Sessions are delivered in person across the South and nationally, and virtually.

  • Keynote

    30 to 45 minutes, with or without moderated question time. Built for plenary and general sessions.

  • Breakout or workshop

    60 to 90 minutes, participatory. Attendees leave with a framework or instrument they can use, not just notes.

  • Panel participation or moderation

    Available as a panelist or as moderator. Moderation is a trained skill, and it is one of hers.

  • Board or leadership briefing

    45 to 60 minutes, closed. Tailored to a decision the body is currently facing, with candid discussion off the record.

  • Private institutional briefing

    Confidential sessions for agencies, coalitions, and executive teams working on a live issue. Content built from the client's own context.

  • Training and facilitation

    Half-day and full-day sessions on qualitative methods, listening session design, and facilitation practice.

About the Speaker

Tania Johns, MPA

Founder and Principal, PRC Impact · Qualitative Researcher · Civic Strategist · RIVA-Trained Moderator

Tania Johns leads qualitative research and civic strategy for organizations making decisions that affect communities, public money, and public trust. Her work spans public investment, health communications, violence prevention planning, voter engagement, and community planning.

She led qualitative research and civic strategy supporting the 2020 Montgomery County school property tax referendum — a measure that raised the rate from 10 to 22 mills in a state where school tax measures routinely fail, passing with 61.1 percent of the vote and generating roughly $33 million a year for Montgomery Public Schools. She has conducted community listening on violence prevention in Birmingham and national in-depth interviews with prescribers on health communications.

Her civic background includes serving as Deputy Campaign Manager for the historic campaign that elected Montgomery's first Black mayor, prior experience at a national polling and public opinion research firm, and leadership roles in multi-state civic engagement work.

She is a RIVA-trained focus group moderator, a graduate of Alabama State University, and holds a Master of Public Administration from Auburn University at Montgomery with a concentration in healthcare administration and policy. Her AI training includes certification in AI consulting, prompt design, and instructional design, with ongoing study in responsible AI frameworks including the NIST AI Risk Management Framework and the EU AI Act.

She is based in Montgomery, Alabama.

Organizer Questions

What planners usually need to know before bringing a proposal to a committee.

What does an honorarium look like?

Honoraria vary by format, preparation required, audience, and travel. Tell us the event, the format, and your budget range, and we will tell you directly whether it works. We do not waste a planner's time with a long negotiation.

Will she travel?

Yes. She speaks in person across Alabama and the South and nationally, and virtually when that serves the audience better. Travel and lodging are handled separately from the honorarium for events outside the Montgomery area.

Can the talk be customized to our audience?

Yes, and it should be. Every session is adapted to the audience and the decision they are facing. For institutional clients, sessions can be built from the client's own context and research rather than from general material.

How far in advance should we reach out?

Earlier is better for keynotes and custom sessions, since preparation is part of the work. Shorter timelines are sometimes possible for panels, moderation, and briefings.

What do you need from us?

The event name and date, audience type and expected size, session format and length, location or platform, and the outcome you want the audience to leave with. Standard bio, headshot, and session description are provided on confirmation.

Does she take a position on data centers or other live political issues?

She speaks as a researcher, not an advocate. Sessions present what communities are actually saying and what a credible public process requires. That holds whether the room is pursuing a project, opposing one, or deciding.

Can sessions be recorded?

Public sessions, generally yes, with agreement in advance. Closed board and institutional briefings are not recorded, which is part of why they are useful.
