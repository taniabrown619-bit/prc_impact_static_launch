Testing Survey Questions Before Launch

Pretest individual questions to see whether people understand and answer them as intended. Then run a pilot survey to check the whole process, from the invitation and skip logic to the exported responses.

Prefer to read?

Would you send a survey without checking how people understand the questions? Think of it like a dress rehearsal. You need to hear the lines clearly and make sure the whole show runs.

Start with a question pretest. Imagine a school asking whether notices are clear and timely. A parent may understand the notice perfectly and still get it too late. Ask what the question meant to them. Then split it into two questions so each answer tells you what to fix.

Next, pilot the whole survey. Send the invitation to a small group from the intended audience. Open it on a phone. Choose “I did not receive a notice” and check that it skips the clarity question. Then open the results file and make sure the skipped answer is blank. Fix the critical issues and run those paths again.

01 / Pretest the questions

Find out what people think you asked

When pretesting a questionnaire, ask someone from the intended audience to answer each priority question before you explain it. This is one way to use cognitive testing for survey questions. Ask neutral follow-ups to find out how they understood the question, remembered relevant events and chose an answer. Read the U.S. Census Bureau’s guide to questionnaire testing methods for more on cognitive interviewing and field testing.

Three neutral probes. “What does this question mean in your own words?” “What did you think about when you chose that answer?” “Which answer option came closest, and what was missing?” Ask about an unfamiliar term only after the participant has tried to answer on their own.

First draft

“Are school notices clear and timely?”

A parent can understand a notice and still receive it too late. One answer hides which problem to fix.

Revised questions

“How easy was it to understand what the most recent notice asked you to do?”

“Did you receive that notice in time to act on it?” Give respondents a separate option for “I did not receive a notice.”

02 / Run a small trial

A pilot survey example from invitation to export

Imagine a school district preparing a short survey about family notices. After revising the wording, the team sends the survey to a small group of intended respondents using the invitation and survey link it plans to use at launch. It includes people who received a notice and people who did not, so both routes through the questionnaire get tested.

  1. Open the invitation

    Use the real sender name, links and contact information. Try the invitation on a phone and with keyboard navigation.

  2. Exercise every route

    Select “I did not receive a notice.” Check that the survey skips the clarity question and lets that person finish. Test the translated version and any other planned mode.

  3. Check the data

    Compare several submitted test responses with the export. Verify answer labels, intentional blanks, time stamps and the confirmation that respondents see.

Try a few paths intentionally as a team, then observe what intended respondents encounter on their own. Keep pilot records separate from the live survey if questions, options or routing change. For help choosing what to ask families, see the parent and family engagement surveys guide.

03 / Decide whether to launch

Log, fix and retest the failures that matter

Record the exact question or path, what happened, who will fix it and how you will check the fix. Prioritize errors that change how people answer, block completion or corrupt the export.

Example log entry. On the mobile survey, respondents who selected “I did not receive a notice” still saw a required clarity question. The survey owner updates the branch rule. A second tester reruns both notice routes and checks that the skipped field is blank in the export.

Schedule launch after the critical paths work, the priority questions are understood as intended, and someone has checked the results file. Carry unresolved issues into the launch decision with an owner and a plan.

Common questions

What is the difference between a survey pretest and a pilot survey?

Use a question pretest to investigate how people understand and answer individual items. Use a pilot survey to see whether the questionnaire and the entire process work together, from the invitation to the data export.

How many people should take a pilot survey?

Plan around what needs testing. Cover the main language, device, accessibility and question paths, then add testers if the first round misses a priority audience or a critical branch. A pilot is a check on the survey process.

Can I use a pilot to estimate public opinion?

Use pilot feedback to improve the instrument. For population estimates, plan sampling, fieldwork and interpretation separately. See the public opinion and voter research guide for how qualitative findings can inform that work.
