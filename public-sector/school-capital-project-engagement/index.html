What should school districts tell families after a capital project is funded? Start with three things: what has been approved, what families can still influence, and when they will hear from you again. A funded project still needs a school-by-school communication plan.

Imagine construction will move a school's entrance. Share a dated map with the new route, the start date, and who families can contact. Ask which parts of the plan are difficult for people using the bus, walking to school, or using accessible parking.

Offer more than one way to respond. A campus forum can surface questions quickly. A written follow-up gives families who cannot attend a chance to weigh in. Then report back: what changed, what is still open, and when you will share the next update.

On Montgomery Public Schools' capital program, PRC Impact supported community forums and public communications as a subconsultant to Volkert. Use the campus checklist to plan your next update. If your district wants help planning community engagement, start a project at prcimpact.com.

Set the decision boundary

Start by separating fixed decisions from open questions. The funded scope, board approvals or building limits may already be settled. Ask families about choices the project team can still consider, such as where to share construction updates, what routes create access problems, or which unanswered questions need an explanation.

For example, if the school site and budget are set, say so in the invitation. Ask people to identify drop-off and walking concerns during a planned phase. Tell them who reviews the feedback and when the response will be published.

Plan campus forums and other ways to hear from families

Give each school the same simple brief: the project, current stage, next milestone and open questions. Use school-specific maps or schedules only when the district can explain which details are current. Offer more than one way to respond so families who cannot attend still have a usable route.

A forum can surface practical problems quickly. Use a short written follow-up for families who need translation, cannot attend, or want time to respond. Group questions by school, phase and owner before turning them into an action list.

Montgomery Public Schools Capital Improvement Plan

Report back as construction plans change

Keep a public update record with the question or concern, the response, the date and the next milestone. Publish what changed, what stayed the same and why, and what is still under review. Update earlier materials when the current plan differs so residents can identify the latest information.

For example, if a temporary entrance moves, publish the new route, effective date and contact point together. If a schedule is uncertain, give the next date when the district expects to know more.

Questions to consider

What can families influence after funding is approved?

It depends on the district's current decisions. Name the choices still open, identify who can act on them and explain the parts already approved. Give families a separate place to ask for an explanation of fixed decisions.

Is a public meeting enough?

A meeting helps when people can ask the project team direct questions. Also offer a way to respond outside the meeting and follow up in the places families already get district updates.

How can a district reach families affected by the work who miss the town hall?

Use the channels families already check, offer accessible and translated materials where needed, and give families a practical way to ask a question after the meeting. Put the date, campus, change and next update together.
