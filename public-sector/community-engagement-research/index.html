How do you turn a community meeting into useful research? Start with the decision, not the event.

Describe what people can influence, what is already fixed, and who will make the final choice. More meetings do not automatically mean more influence.

Next, ask whose experience could be missing. A convenient meeting time for your team may not work for the people you need to hear from.

For example, an organization reviewing a service could combine a small discussion with individual interviews. That is an illustrative planning example, not a PRC project result.

Explain how the findings will be considered, and report back on what changed and what could not change. Keep participation separate from a promise to adopt every idea.

Direct answer

What is community engagement research?

It is a structured way to hear from people affected by a decision, interpret their experiences and show how their input informed the next step.

Research-led engagement is not just a well-attended meeting. It connects the choice an organization faces, the people it needs to hear from, the listening method and the use of the findings. Our work includes community and stakeholder engagement, qualitative research, focus groups, interviews, listening sessions, analysis and recommendations.

The U.S. Environmental Protection Agency's public participation guide advises teams to choose a participation level that matches the public's actual influence. That is independent methods guidance, not a PRC endorsement or a rule for every project.

A decision-led path

From invitation to usable direction.

Each engagement should be scoped to its decision, audience and time frame. These are planning questions, not a promise of preset methods or deliverables.

Name the decision

Identify who will decide, when the choice will be made and which parts are still open. Explain the boundary in plain language before asking for input.

Identify the people

Ask whose experience is most affected and who may be absent from an easy-to-reach audience. Access, timing, language and trust can all shape participation.

Fit the method

A forum may help people hear one another; an interview may make space for a detailed or sensitive account. Focus groups and listening sessions answer different questions. Select methods for the question, not for a predetermined session count.

Interpret and report back

Separate what participants said from the team's interpretation, note meaningful differences and describe how findings were used. Do not treat a small qualitative group as a population-wide vote.

For public agencies

How can research support a government decision?

It can show what affected people understand, experience and question before an agency makes its choice. The agency remains responsible for the decision and for explaining how public input was considered.

Match participation to real influence

Start by naming what is open to change. If the decision is already fixed, invite questions and explain it clearly instead of promising that a listening session can change the outcome.

Choose a method for the evidence needed

Government focus groups can explore reasons and language in depth. Open forums can bring multiple perspectives into one discussion. Neither method alone measures the views of an entire community; recruitment and reporting should make that limit clear.

The EPA public participation guide recommends matching the participation level to the public's actual opportunity to influence a decision. This is general methods guidance, not a rule that every agency must use the same process.

Prepare a useful inquiry

What should an engagement brief include?

Bring the decision even if the method is not settled. PRC can discuss the research role and scope with your team.

  1. Decision: what choice will the findings inform, and who owns it?
  2. Influence: what can participants shape, and what is fixed?
  3. People: which communities or stakeholders need to be heard?
  4. Evidence: what is already known and what remains uncertain?
  5. Timing: when is the decision, and what review steps are required?
  6. Outputs: what kind of findings or recommendations would be useful?

What should a community engagement RFP ask for?

Describe the public decision, intended participants, actual opportunity to influence the result and known constraints. Ask respondents to explain how they would recruit people, make participation accessible, document differing perspectives, analyze the input and report findings with limitations. Name the agency's decision owner, timetable, review steps and expected deliverables. Your procurement team should set the formal solicitation and data-handling requirements for its jurisdiction.

For a larger study design, see PRC's qualitative research studies guide. For testing public wording, see message and issue research.

Practical questions

Questions before you invite participation.

Use these answers to frame the work before inviting participation.

How is engagement different from a survey?

Engagement describes a relationship and process around a decision. A survey is one possible research method. A project can use both, but method choice depends on what the organization needs to learn and what participants can influence.

Does a forum represent everyone?

No. People who attend may differ from those who cannot or choose not to. A qualitative forum can surface reasons and perspectives, but it does not by itself estimate the views of an entire population.

What should happen after listening?

The commissioning team should be able to describe the themes, differences and limits of the evidence and explain which next steps were influenced by it. The exact reporting and decision responsibilities belong in the project scope.

We can discuss research and engagement support within an existing plan. Agree the role, methods and deliverables for your project.

Practical decision aid

Turn a meeting request into a decision brief.

Before choosing a format, put the decision on one page. A useful brief distinguishes choices that participants can influence from constraints the commissioning team must explain. It also names who will interpret the evidence and who will make the decision.

Decision owner

Write down the choice, its owner and the date by which findings can still help. If the outcome is fixed, explain the decision rather than asking for input that cannot affect it.

Participation gap

List whose experience is needed and who the simplest invitation would miss. Identify a practical alternative for people who cannot use the proposed time, language or format.

Return path

Decide who will report back, what they can report and when. Separate a theme in the research from a commitment to adopt a proposal.

What to clarify before commissioning

For an initial project conversation, share the decision, audience, timeline and existing evidence. Costs and delivery timing depend on recruitment, access needs, method mix and reporting requirements; agree those responsibilities before fieldwork. Do not send private participant records in an initial inquiry.
